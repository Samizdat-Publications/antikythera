# Accuracy check against Freeth et al. 2021 (18 October 2026)

**Paper:** Freeth, Higgon, Dacanalis, MacDonald, Georgakopoulou and Wojcik, "A Model of the Cosmos in the
ancient Greek Antikythera Mechanism", *Scientific Reports* 11:5821 (2021), doi 10.1038/s41598-021-84310-w,
read from nature.com together with its Supplementary Information (the corrected SI file, MOESM4). Figure 3
(the gear-train tables 3e and 3f) was read from the figure image itself.

**Checked:** `data/gears.json` (every tooth count, mesh, coupling and expected rate), `blender/dials.py`
(the front display), `web/src/ui/inspector.ts`, the walkthrough in `web/src/ui/onboarding.ts`, and the
landing page `site/index.html`. Every planetary ratio was recomputed independently from the tooth counts
in `gears.json` with exact fractions and compared with Fig. 3e/3f; both test suites pass (36 Python, 34 web).

**Verdict:** every tooth count, gear train, period relation, pin-and-slot offset and pin distance in the
app matches the paper. The differences are in the drawing of the front display, in geometry that does
not touch any ratio, and in a few places where the paper is silent and the app had to choose. Five
pieces of reader-facing text overstated or misstated things; they are fixed and deployed.

## 5. Text checked and left as is

Walkthrough leaves 1 to 14 and the landing rooms otherwise agree with the paper or with the repo's
other sources: 82 fragments, a third surviving, 30 gears (27 in A, one each in B, C, D); 48 on 223 at the
crank; 64/38 · 48/24 · 127/32 = 254/19; 1.1 mm pin-and-slot on a 223-tooth platform turning once in 8.88
years; the Egyptian calendar drawn with 365 days as the paper has it; 462 and 442 on the front cover
inscription; Mars with the largest offset. The 51 glyphs (38 lunar, 28 solar) were confirmed in Freeth 2014
(PLOS ONE 9:e103275). Leaf 12 says each body sits where "its own pin-and-slot" puts it; for Mercury, Venus
and the Sun it is a pin and slotted follower, which the paper writes with the same symbol, so it was left.

## 6. Not verified in this pass

* The exact epoch wording: Freeth 2014 runs its sequence from 12 May 205 BC, but I could not open
  Carman and Evans 2014 to confirm that they name the same day; nor Voulgaris et al. 2023 for 22 Dec 178 BC.
* The back-train gear counts and dial directions against Freeth 2006 and 2008 (not in the 2021 paper).
* The 2024 calendar-ring recount (Woan and Bayley) and the 2025 preprint (Szigety and Arenas); cited as the
  repo already had them.
* The app labels the Voulgaris epoch "Voulgaris et al. 2022" (`web/src/mech/mechanism.ts:31`) while the
  landing page and NOTES give Almagest 14.1 (2023); I could not check which year the authors prefer.
* `a1`'s internal note ("only its keyway survives"); Table S8 lists a1 as a surviving contrate gear without
  saying how much remains. The note is not shown to visitors.

## 7. Worth knowing before writing to the authors

* The SI caption to Supplementary Fig. S22(e) gives the Mercury epicyclic pair as "40 ~ 24", while Fig. 3e,
  the main text and SI 5.4 all give 40 ~ 20. Only 20 yields (1513, 480); the app uses 20.
* The Mars period relation in the paper is (133, 284), 133 synodic cycles in 284 years. 71 is the tooth
  count of the Mars pin gear, not the period's year count.
