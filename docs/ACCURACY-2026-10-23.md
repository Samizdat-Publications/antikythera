# Second accuracy pass: a cross-check by another model, and what it found (23 October 2026)

Before writing to the Freeth team, the repository was handed to a second model (Google's Gemini 4 Argon,
with Google Search) as a referee: the gear table, the walkthrough and inspector text, the epoch and
astronomy code, and the visible text of the exhibit and the landing page, with the 18 October ledger
(`docs/ACCURACY-2026-10-18.md`) and the instruction to look for what that pass missed. Every finding was
then checked here against the code, a measurement, or a source before anything changed. A second run
watched a screen recording of the fourteen narrated leaves and the main views.

## 1. Accepted and fixed

| Finding | Checked how | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **The 3D Moon pointer ran up to 20° off the sky.** The lunar pin-and-slot (k1 on e3) was never phase-fitted, so in the model its anomaly ran roughly opposite to the real one; the column, computed separately, was right. | Measured in the running exhibit: 3D minus sky swung from -5° to +20° each anomalistic month, the column stayed within 2°. | `web/src/astro/phases.ts` fits k1 like the planets' pins. Now 3D and column agree within 0.75°, and the 3D Moon is within about 3° of the sky near the epoch (fit: rms 0.9°, worst 1.8°). |
| **The Voulgaris epoch broke the eclipse audit.** The Saros and Metonic dials were set as if every epoch were a full moon. 22 Dec 178 BC is a new moon, so the cell index rounded across a half month: the exhibit showed a lunar glyph (cell 120) on the day of the annular solar eclipse. | Audit of one Saros from 22 Dec 178 BC: 0 of 38 lunar and 0 of 28 solar glyphs hit, 66 false alarms. | `calibrate` keeps the cell as a fraction. The Voulgaris epoch now lands 0.96 of the way through cell 119, a surviving solar glyph (Freeth 2014 Table S3B), with NASA's annular eclipse that day "a hit"; its audit is 28 of 28 solar, as the 205 BC epoch's is. Pinned by `web/src/astro/calibration.test.ts`. |
| **The Saros and Metonic pointers stood half a month off.** In Freeth's Eclipse Year Model a month opens at the first crescent, with the full moon at 17 of its 38 units and the new moon at 36. The app opened each cell at the full moon. | `eym.ts` constants against `calibrate`. | Cell 1 now opens 17/38 of a month before 12 May 205 BC; the eclipse panel, the chime and the audit take each cell's full moon at 17/38. The C&E audit is unchanged (27 lunar hits, 10 penumbral only, 1 false alarm; 28 of 28 solar). |
| **The lunar apogee was assumed, not measured.** `apogee0` was set to the Moon's own longitude ("full moon near apogee"). | Moon error over a year either side: rms 1.39° vs 1.27° (205 BC), 1.29° vs 1.16° (178 BC). | `apogee0` is the Moon's apogee on the epoch day from the ephemeris. |
| **The zodiac and parapegma readout used the mean Sun.** On the bronze the true-Sun pointer reads the zodiac and the parapegma letters (Freeth 2021, SI 6.4). | Code read. | The front column's Sun row and the parapegma row now read the true-Sun pointer; the mean Sun is a sub-row labelled as the date pointer's. |
| **Credit for 12 May 205 BC.** Freeth's own 2014 PLOS ONE paper reached the same epoch as Carman and Evans in the same year. The app credited only Carman and Evans. | Carman and Evans 2014 (AHES 68) and Freeth 2014 (PLOS ONE 9: e103275). | Walkthrough leaf 5, its narration, the epoch menu, the colophon, the landing page and the README credit both. |
| **"A 2024 recount of the holes."** Woan and Bayley did not recount: they analysed, statistically, the hole positions Budiselic and colleagues measured (2020), and found 354 or 355. | Press release and coverage of the Horological Journal paper (July 2024). | "The holes beneath the ring, measured in 2020 and analysed statistically in 2024, point to 354"; Budiselic et al. 2020 added to the sources. |
| **"Teeth as uneven as those measured."** Szigety and Arenas (2025 preprint) simulated the tooth errors Edmunds estimated in 2011 (JHA 42); they did not measure teeth. | arXiv 2504.00327 and its coverage. | Leaf 13, its narration and the landing page say so and cite Edmunds 2011. |
| **Voulgaris et al. "2022" in the epoch menu**, 2023 everywhere else. | The paper is Almagest 14.1 (2023); 2022 was the arXiv preprint. | 2023. |

## 2. Found here while checking

| Finding | Fix |
|---|---|
| Leaf 13 said the Moon "stays within about two degrees". Measured: within about 3.5° for some years either side of the epoch, then a drift of about a degree every eighteen years, because 254/19 sidereal months a year is 0.056° a year faster than the Moon (with a turn taken as a tropical year). Over ±40 years the worst is 6.5°. | Leaf 13, its narration, the landing page and the README now say this, and that the mean Sun keeps time by construction because a turn is taken as a tropical year. The chart's Sun band is labelled "mean Sun". |
| The Moon panel said the machine's Moon is "lit from where its Sun pointer stands"; the phase ball takes the mean Sun (SI 6.2.4). | "lit from the mean Sun, as in the 2021 model". |
| The README's walkthrough stills (and the old project page's clips) were filmed before the 18 October text fixes, so leaf 7 still showed "between the sun and moon pointers". | The fourteen README stills re-shot from the fixed build. The old project page's animated clips are unchanged (that page is no longer linked). |

## 2b. From the video critique (the walkthrough and the views, watched by the same model)

The fourteen leaves were filmed off the fixed build at about four frames a second, with Arthur's clips laid
back in at the moment each leaf opened, and the main views and themes as a silent reel.

| Finding | Fix |
|---|---|
| Leaf 10 talks about the Saros spiral and the Exeligmos dial while the walkthrough card covers their lower half. | A `saros` camera that holds both above the card. |
| Leaf 4 describes the crown wheel against the main wheel while the camera looks at the closed case's side. | The leaf shows only a1 and b1 (the case opens for an isolated train) from a `crank-input` camera. |
| In leaves 1, 5 and 13 the idle orbit swung the camera round to the case's blank side mid-sentence. | `Viewer.holdOrbit`: no idle orbit while the walkthrough is open. |
| "Point to 354" reads as settled; the 2021 model keeps 365. | "suggest 354" (leaf 6, its narration, the landing page). |
| Going back a leaf could carry an isolated train or Inside into the front-dial leaves. | Leaves 5, 6, 7 and 10 reset both. |

Not taken: the case "floating" above the plinth with Inside on, the parapegma and glyph legibility at this
distance, card text and narration not word for word (the narration spells numbers out on purpose), and turning
the epoch on screen during leaf 5. Leaf 13 now runs 86 seconds; a shorter version is a taste call.

## 3. Rejected, with the reason

| Finding | Why not |
|---|---|
| "The Callippic dial and its four gears were dropped in 2008; the 2021 model has 31 gears behind b1, not 35." (made by both runs, the second time as its top recommendation) | Freeth et al. 2008 identified the Olympiad (Games) dial *and* kept a Callippic dial on the strength of the "76" in the back-plate inscription: its supplementary notes have a section "Metonic, Olympiad & Callippic Dials". The 18 October pass checked the 35 + 34 = 69 count against the 2021 main text and SI 6.3. |
| "Map a turn of b1 to 365.247 days, not the tropical year." | A modelling choice, not an error; the exhibit now says which year it uses and what that does to the Moon. |
| "Tony Freeth is a co-author of Budiselic et al. 2020." | Not verified; not claimed anywhere. |

## 4. Still not verified

* Whether Voulgaris and colleagues number the cell of the 178 BC eclipse as cell 1 (their "renumbering"); the
  exhibit keeps Freeth's numbering and glyph placement for both epochs; the epoch only sets where the pointer stands.
* The back-train counts against Freeth 2006 and 2008 directly (the referee's arithmetic agrees:
  Metonic 5/19, Games -1/4, e3 -477/4237, Saros -940/4237, Exeligmos -235/12711 per year).
* `a1`'s internal note ("only its keyway survives"); the referee says the crown gear itself survives. Not shown
  to visitors.
