# Third accuracy pass: corrections from Tony Freeth (29 October 2026)

The outreach email of 23 October went to Tony Freeth, lead author of the 2021 Cosmos model this
exhibit follows. He replied on 29 October with corrections. They outrank both earlier passes
(`docs/ACCURACY-2026-10-18.md`, `docs/ACCURACY-2026-10-23.md`) and the AI referee. Each was then
checked against the 2021 paper and its Supplementary Information, Freeth et al. 2008 (Nature 454,
Supplementary Notes) and the machine itself before anything changed. The version before this pass is
tagged `v1.2-pre-freeth-review`.

His reply also confirmed the slip reported in the email: the caption of Supplementary Fig. S22(e) gives
the Mercury pair as 40 ~ 24, where Fig. 3e, the main text and SI section 5.4 give 40 ~ 20.

## 1. Corrected

| Correction | Checked how | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Who first saw the gears.** Spyridon Stais noticed the gearwheels in Fragment A in May 1902, not his cousin Valerios, who was the museum's director. | Jones, *A Portable Cosmos* (2017), the source Freeth gave. | Walkthrough leaf 2, its narration, the landing page. |
| **The input crown gear was wrong, and turned the wrong way.** a1 sat level with b1, clear of its rim, so it never met b1's teeth, and its rate had the spur sign, -223/48. | 2021 SI Figs. S10 and S11 put a1 on b1's front face at its right-hand rim, upright beside the long pillar, its axle radial. A crown gear standing on a wheel's face and meeting its rim at its lowest tooth is carried round in the same sense: +223/48. | `data/gears.json` gives a1 its module (0.578, b1's) and `crown_same_sense`; `python/mech/ratios.py` honours it; `blender/build_all.py` stands a1 on b1's face, its lowest tooth in b1's teeth. |
| **The rings were the wrong widths.** The Sun ring is much wider, as are the calendar and zodiac rings. | Fig. 7 measured by radial profile with the calendar's outer edge taken as 80 mm: calendar 74.1 to 80, zodiac 66.7 to 73.9, date 64.2 to 66.5, Saturn 59.7 to 63.9, Jupiter 54.9 to 59.4, Mars 50.1 to 54.6, true Sun 43.6 to 49.8, Venus 38.7 to 43.3, Mercury 34.3 to 38.4 mm. | The front display is rebuilt to those radii (`FRONT_RINGS` in `tools/gen_dial_textures.py`, carried to `blender/dials.py` in `dials.json`). |
| **The front display was simplified.** The Sun and the date were plain pointers. | Fig. 7 and its caption. | The true Sun is a ring carrying the Age of the Moon scale (days 1 to 29, clockwise from the Sun, read by the Moon pointer) and the wider and narrower eclipse-limit triangles (20 and 7 of the Eclipse Year Model's 223 units), with the golden sphere and its pointer. The date is a narrow ring with a short Date pointer. Superior planets have opposition markers. The Dragon Hand has a full tail. A raised central disc carries the Earth's dome, and the Moon pointer carries the phase ball, which had been buried inside the machine. |
| **The index letters were missing.** | Fig. 8 and the section "Indexing of synodic events to planetary rings": one alphabet through the synodic events, read by the Sun pointer on each ring. | Each ring carries a scale mark and letter for each event: Mercury Α to Ζ (Α, Β, Γ faint, since those lines of the inscription are lost), Venus Η to Μ, Mars Ν to Π, Jupiter Ρ to Υ, Saturn Φ to Ω. The paper gives no angles, so each mark is where this machine puts the Sun on that ring at the event, averaged over 40 years. |
| **The back pointers did not match the evidence.** They overhung the dials and carried counterweight tails. | 2008 SI reconstructions of the back plate. | Both spiral pointers now end at the outer edge of the last turn of cells, with no tail; the subsidiary pointers lost their tails too. |
| **The spirals started in the wrong place.** Both the Metonic and Saros scales start at the top. | Freeth's correction; 2008 SI Fig. 15 and 25 for the Saros. | Both spirals open at the top and run clockwise seen from the back. Measuring the old pointers in the running exhibit also showed they had not stood in the cells the exhibit's own panel named (at the 205 BC epoch the Saros pointer was half a turn away, the Metonic about four months): their offsets had the wrong sense for a dial drawn seen from the back. The offsets are now derived from the dial geometry (`web/src/astro/calibration.ts`). |
| **The Olympiad dial.** | 2008 SI: the quarters are turned about 8° anticlockwise from square, year 1 lower right, numbered anticlockwise, the way its pointer turns. | Redrawn to match. The Callippic quarters are numbered clockwise, the way its pointer turns (no physical evidence survives for that dial; it is kept as the 2021 model has it). |
| **The 354-hole theory was quoted as if correct.** | Freeth's correction. | The exhibit, its narration and the landing page now say the 365-day ring is the 2021 model's, and that a reading of the holes as 354 is disputed. |
| **Planet accuracy was overstated.** Simple epicyclic theories go wrong at retrograde motion, by more than a zodiac sign within a few years, and predict evenly spaced retrograde loops; the long-term cycles are accurate. | Measured on this machine against astronomy-engine, from the 205 BC epoch: worst error within 2 years Mercury 12°, Venus 11°, Mars 9°, Jupiter 3°, Saturn 2°; within 10 years Mars 54°. Mars's retrograde loops recur every 2.134 to 2.136 years on the machine, every 2.09 to 2.21 years in the sky. | Leaf 13 (How good was it?), its narration, and a new figure on the landing page. |

## 2. Not changed

* The epochs, the gear table and every rate other than a1's sign: none of the corrections touched them.
* The phase ball's own gear, q1, still sits where b0 drives it. The ball is now drawn on the Moon pointer
  as in Fig. 7 and turned at q1's rate, without the axle between them. This is listed among the
  departures on the landing page.
