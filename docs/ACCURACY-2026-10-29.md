# Third accuracy pass: expert corrections, and an audit against the figures (29 October 2026)

The outreach email of 23 October went to Tony Freeth, lead author of the 2021 Cosmos model this exhibit
follows, and he kindly replied on 29 October with corrections. They outrank both earlier passes
(`docs/ACCURACY-2026-10-18.md`, `docs/ACCURACY-2026-10-23.md`) and the AI referee. Each was checked against the
2021 paper and its Supplementary Information, Freeth et al. 2008 (Nature 454, Supplementary Notes) and Freeth
2014 (PLOS ONE 9, e103275) before anything changed. The version before this pass is tagged `v1.2-pre-freeth-review`.

After those fixes the whole model was audited again, this time against the papers' figures rather than their
numbers alone: two reviewers each asked to read the model as the 2021 team would (one for the front and the
crank, one for the back dials and every published sentence), and Gemini 4 Argon as a third. Every finding below
was verified against the sources; the ones that were not (for example, that the Saros spiral starts at the
bottom, or that its solar glyphs number 27, the 2008 count that Freeth 2014 revised to 28) were set aside.

## 1. The corrections

| Correction | Checked how | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Who first saw the gears.** Spyridon Stais noticed the gearwheels in Fragment A in May 1902, not his cousin Valerios, who was the museum's director. | Jones, *A Portable Cosmos* (2017). | Walkthrough leaf 2, its narration, the landing page. |
| **The input crown gear made no sense and turned the wrong way.** a1 sat level with b1, clear of its rim, and had the spur sign, -223/48. | 2021 SI Figs. S10 and S11 put a1 on b1's front face at its right-hand rim, upright, its axle radial. Standing on the face and meeting the rim at its lowest tooth, it is carried round the same way: +223/48. | `gears.json` (module 0.578, `crown_same_sense`), `ratios.py`, `build_all.py`; `test_input_crown_gear`. |
| **The rings were too wide.** | Fig. 7 measured by radial profile, the calendar's outer edge taken as 80 mm: calendar 74.1 to 80, zodiac 66.7 to 73.9, date 64.2 to 66.5, Saturn 59.7 to 63.9, Jupiter 54.9 to 59.4, Mars 50.1 to 54.6, true Sun 43.6 to 49.8, Venus 38.7 to 43.3, Mercury 34.3 to 38.4 mm. A second, independent measurement agreed within half a millimetre. | `FRONT_RINGS` in `tools/gen_dial_textures.py`. |
| **The front display was simplified.** | Fig. 7 and its caption; 2021 SI 2.3.1 and 6.3. | The true Sun is a ring with the golden sphere, its ray and pointer, the Age of the Moon scale and the eclipse-limit triangles; the date a narrow ring with a short Date pointer; opposition markers; a full Dragon Hand; the Earth's dome. |
| **The index letters were omitted.** | Fig. 8 and the section "Indexing of synodic events to planetary rings". | Mercury Α to Ζ (Α, Β, Γ faint, those lines being lost), Venus Η to Μ, Mars Ν to Π, Jupiter Ρ to Υ, Saturn Φ to Ω, each beside a scale mark where the Sun pointer stands at the event. As the SI proposes (6.4.6), the marks are found with the machine itself. |
| **The back-dial pointers did not conform to the evidence.** | Freeth 2014, Fig. 4: the Saros pointer is a bar sliding through a guide on its arbor, the follower under its tip, the other end projecting past the hub. | Both spiral pointers are now built that way; the follower rides in the groove in the turn of the current cell and moves outward as the months pass (before, it began on the innermost turn whatever the date, and moved inward). The subsidiary pointers end at their dials' rims. |
| **The Metonic and Saros scales start at the top.** | Freeth 2014 Fig. 4 and 2008 SI Fig. 25 for the Saros. (The 2008 SI's Fig. 15 began the Metonic at the inside bottom; this copy follows the correction.) | Both spirals open at the top and run clockwise seen from the back. The old pointer offsets also ignored that the back dials are drawn as seen from the back, so the pointers had not stood in the cells the panel named; they are now derived from the dial geometry. |
| **The 354-hole theory was quoted as if correct.** | | The exhibit, narration and landing page call the 365-day ring the 2021 model's and the 354 reading disputed. |
| **Planet accuracy was overstated.** Simple epicyclic theories go wrong at retrograde motion by more than a sign within a few years and predict evenly spaced loops; the long-term cycles are accurate. | Measured on this machine against astronomy-engine from the 205 BC epoch. Within 2 years the worst errors are Mercury 12°, Venus 11°, Mars 9°, Jupiter 3°, Saturn 2°, small only because each pointer is fitted to the sky at the epoch; within 10 years Mars reaches 54°. Mars's loops recur every 2.134 to 2.136 years on the machine, every 2.09 to 2.21 in the sky. | Leaf 13, its narration, and a new figure on the landing page. |

## 2. Found in the audit and fixed

| Finding | Source | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **The Zodiac's zero was at 3 o'clock.** | The parapegma plate above the dial carries Capricorn to Gemini and the one below Libra to Sagittarius and Cancer to Virgo, each column beside "the adjacent quarter of the Zodiac Dial" (2021 SI); Fig. 7. | 0° of Aries now stands at the top; every front pointer reads from there. |
| **The Egyptian calendar ring was never set.** The Date pointer did not point at the date the panel gave. | 2021 SI 6.4.5; 2008 SI 3.5 (the ring moved back a day for leap years). | The ring is turned so the Date pointer reads the epoch's day, and slipped back a day every four years. |
| **The Moon's phase drive was a stand-in.** The phase ball sat inside the machine, then on the pointer with no gear behind it. | Main text and SI 6.2.6: the mean Sun is carried by a tube next to the lunar arbor to b0, which drives the crown gear of the lunar phase device. | A mean-Sun tube runs from b1 up the centre to b0, at the front under the Earth's dome; b0 turns q1, whose axle carries the ball out along the Moon's arm, as in Fig. 7. |
| **The layers between the Strap and the CP were reversed.** | 2021 SI S6: "from the Strap to the CP are the true Sun, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn"; the true Sun's follower "on the other side of the CP". | Reordered; the nested tubes now reach back to their own output wheels. |
| **The Metonic months ignored the 13-month years**, and the dial had no year numbers or excluded-day numerals. | 2008 SI 3.2 and 3.3: 13-month years 1, 3, 6, 9, 11, 14, 17; the L-shaped year sign in each year's first cell; excluded-day numerals under cells 1 to 45; names over two or three lines, as Fig. 15 draws them. | All drawn; the panel names months from the same table; `test_metonic.py` holds the structure to the SI's constraints. |
| **The calendars' starting points were arbitrary**: Meton's summer of 432 BC and the first Olympiad. | 2008 SI 3.5: the Corinthian year begins in autumn; the Olympiad pointer points down at the start of Metonic year 1. | A cycle begins with the first new Moon after the autumnal equinox of 221 BC; the Games pointer runs from there (declared on the landing page). |
| **The Olympiad dial was laid out inside out**: the games inside the rim, the years at its edge. | 2008 SI 3.5 and Fig. 15: year numbers with the L sign inside the rim, the games outside it, reading outward in the order held. | Redrawn at the figure's size, with the 2008 distance of 24.4 mm from the Metonic centre. |
| **The Callippic dial** was numbered against its pointer and stated as fact. | 2008 SI 3.7: "placed symmetrically to the Olympiad Dial"; 2021 SI: no physical evidence. | Drawn as the Olympiad dial's mirror; called conjectural everywhere. |
| **The Exeligmos dial's sectors were misplaced.** | 2008 SI 4.8 and Fig. 24: H at 7 o'clock, Iϛ at 11 o'clock, a horizontal division; Fig. 25 places the dial above the Saros centre. | Redrawn and moved. |
| **The Saros glyphs** had index letters by cell number, 24-hour times, and invented hours. | 2008 SI 4.1 and 4.4, Freeth 2014: letters in alphabetical order through the glyphs (Glyph 78 is Τ), the second alphabet barred; a 12-hour scale with "of the day" and "of the night"; the reconstructed glyphs carry no times. | All three fixed; the Full Moon Cycle quarter marks added. |
| **Gear names n2 and n3 were swapped.** | 2008 SI Fig. 14: the 57-tooth gear driving the Olympiad dial is n3. | Renamed. |
| **Text.** The glyph count presented as surviving (18 survive; 51 is the 2014 reconstruction); the epoch methods; "a turn of the crank" for a turn of the main wheel; Hipparchus credited with planetary epicycles (2021 SI: he proposed no planetary theory); Mars's stone (the SI's "reddish onyx"); r1's thickness (Table S8: 1.6 mm). | As cited. | Corrected. |

## 3. Open, and not changed

* The index letters in Fig. 7 appear on the opposite side of each planet's stone from where the paper's rule
  ("read by the Sun pointer") puts them. This copy follows the rule.
* The Metonic start: top, as corrected, rather than the 2008 inside bottom.
* b1's frame: the spokes and the bearings the SI ties to them, the four short and four long pillars, and the
  modules the paper sets to seat the Venus and Nodes arbors are this copy's simplification.
* Each planet's pin phase and pointer zero are fitted to the sky separately, which lets an epicycle drift from
  the mean Sun, so the stations do not sit quite symmetrically about opposition on the rings.
