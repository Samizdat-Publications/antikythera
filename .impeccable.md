## Design Context

### Users
A curious general audience with no astronomy background, seeing the machine the way
they would in a museum: standing in front of it while someone (Stewart) explains what
it is. Secondary: enthusiasts and students who want the numbers. The job: understand
what the Antikythera mechanism did, feel the wonder of it, then explore at their own
pace. Desktop and tablet first; mobile only needs to work. Personal showpiece, may be
published publicly; also a demonstration of what an AI-built project can be.

### Brand Personality
Ancient, luminous, exact. The voice of a great museum label: generous, concrete,
never condescending, and every claim checked. Wonder first, rigor on demand.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Museum vitrine.** A warm dark gallery at night; the bronze machine is the exhibit,
lit like one, and everything else recedes the way gallery furniture does. Typography
as inscription: lapidary capitals for titles, a humanist book face for labels and
prose, tabular lining figures for readouts. Panels read as wall labels and catalogue
cards, not app widgets. Bronze, verdigris, lamp-black and vellum, all tinted toward
the bronze hue; no neon, no cyan, no glows, no glass. A future light "Hellenistic
manuscript" theme (parchment, ink, Ptolemaic diagrams) is welcome as an alternate.
Anti-references: generic dark SaaS dashboards, card grids, hero-metric tiles.

### Design Principles
1. The machine is the hero; the interface is gallery lighting.
2. Wonder first, rigor on demand: progressive disclosure for analytics and gear tables.
3. Every number says where it came from and how it was checked.
4. Typography does the branding: inscription, catalogue, ledger.
5. Motion only where the machine moves; nothing decorative animates.

### The second version: Hellenistic manuscript (built 2026-09-13)
The same exhibit as a page from a scholar's copy of Ptolemy: parchment ground with a faint
fibre, iron-gall ink for text and rules, rubrics in red ochre (the small-caps headings and the
one filled button), gold leaf only for the Sun, verdigris ink for the true sky. Type: IM Fell
English SC for rubrics and the title, Cardo (a Bembo revival with Greek) for the text. The 3D
room becomes a scholar's study by day (north-light window, skylight, parchment bounce), the
wall a sheet of vellum; the Sky view and the Moon are drawn as pen diagrams with hatching.
The stage is a numbered plate set into the page inside a double rule, captioned beneath with
how the machine is seen and the date it is set to; sections are parted by a hedera; each leaf of
the walkthrough opens with a red-ochre versal and is numbered in roman. Boxes are ruled, never
rounded. Both versions stay live; the vitrine is the default and must not be changed to suit the
manuscript. Switch: top bar, `?theme=manuscript`, remembered in localStorage.
