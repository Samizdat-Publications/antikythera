The Moon

The Moon as the machine's phase ball has it, lit from where its Sun pointer stands; the face is the near side from NASA's LROC mosaic.

The sky it tracks

Earth in the middle, the zodiac around it. Each body is drawn where its own pin-and-slot puts it, so the trail it leaves is the path Ptolemy would have drawn: the outer planets loop backwards each time the Earth overtakes them. Verdigris ticks are the true sky; bronze ticks are what the machine shows. The strip beneath is the same motion unrolled, longitude against time: where a line steps backwards, that planet is in retrograde.

The front dial what every pointer reads
Against the real sky how far the pointers are from the truth

Truth from astronomy-engine (VSOP87/NOVAS, about an arcminute in the modern era) and NASA's Five Millennium Canon (Espenak & Meeus).

How each anomaly device was set: its pin turned to the phase that best matches the sky over ±1½ of its cycles around the epoch, as the maker would have set it on the day. The error that remains is the model's own: one epicycle cannot follow Mars's eccentric orbit, so Mars wanders most.

How accurate was it? error over the centuries, eclipses hit and missed

Pointer error against the ephemeris, sampled every day: each band is the swing within one year. The ±6.5° pin-and-slot removes most of the Moon's equation of centre; what is left in the band is evection and variation, which the machine does not model.

Saros glyphs (Freeth 2014 eclipse-year model) checked month by month against NASA's canon, from the epoch. A solar glyph counts as a hit if the eclipse was visible somewhere on Earth.
